## Why this exists

The GPL and Apple's App Store terms pull in opposite directions, and have since
VLC was pulled from the store in 2011. The GPL says a recipient may run, copy
and redistribute the program on as many machines as they like; Apple's Developer
Program License Agreement imposes usage rules on the copies it distributes, and
signs the binary with its own DRM. A GPLv3 program submitted with no exception
is therefore distributed under terms its own licence forbids, and Apple has
removed apps on that basis.

Every line of Solvr's own source is written by one author, who holds the
copyright and can grant this. The one third-party component in the app bundle,
[KaTeX](solvr.ai/Resources/KaTeX/LICENSE), is MIT-licensed and imposes no such
restriction.
